For uncertainty resampling and null distributions, see 20 Resampling. For joint error control, see 21 Multiple testing. For study planning, see 22 Sample size. For writing findings, see 23 Reporting.

Methods for complex surveys, survival outcomes, counts, equivalence, multilevel designs, and causal inference require additional treatment beyond this introductory guide.

Appendix B: Design reminders

Term Practical meaning
Independent unit A participant, plot, cluster, or other unit supplying independent information
Paired measurements Two observations linked by the same unit or justified matching
Repeated measurements Several observations from the same unit
Clustered observations Units sharing a higher-level grouping that may induce dependence
Random sampling A sampling mechanism supporting inference to a specified population
Random assignment An allocation mechanism supporting a treatment comparison under additional conditions

Random assignment and random sampling serve different purposes. Neither can be inferred from the appearance of the data table. Unique identifiers do not prove independence.

Appendix C: Glossary

Term Meaning
Parameter A quantity defined for a population or statistical model
Estimator A rule for estimating a parameter from data
Estimate The value obtained by applying that rule
Standard deviation Variation among observations
Standard error Sampling variability of an estimator under the specified design and model
Confidence interval An interval procedure with a stated long-run coverage property under assumptions
Prediction interval An interval for a new observation under a specified prediction model
P-value Probability under the null and other test assumptions of a statistic at least as extreme as observed, under the stated extremeness rule
Effect size Magnitude of a difference or relationship, in original or standardized units
Odds Event probability divided by one minus that probability
Percentage-point difference Absolute difference between percentages
Confounding Distortion of an intended relationship by other variables in the causal structure
Exchangeability Invariance of a joint distribution to specified relabelings under the relevant model or design
Sphericity Equal variances of pairwise contrasts for the repeated conditions in the standard setting
FWER Probability of at least one false rejection within a family
FDR Expected false-discovery proportion, defined as zero when there are no rejections
Power Probability of rejecting the null at a specified alternative under a design and test
Sensitivity analysis Examination of how conclusions change under justified alternative assumptions or decisions

Appendix D: Interpretation checks

If you are about to write… Check first
“No effect” Does the interval rule out effects that matter, or is the estimate imprecise?
“Equivalent” Was a justified equivalence margin and matching procedure used?
“Caused” Does the design and causal argument support that claim?
“Median test” Do the rank method and distributional conditions support a median interpretation?
“Exact” Was the null distribution enumerated or calculated exactly, or only approximated/simulated?
“95% CI” Which construction, parameter, and adjustment status?
“Higher by 20%” Relative percent change or percentage points?
“Adjusted” Covariate adjustment, multiplicity adjustment, or another specific operation?
“Verified by AI” What reproducible checks were actually performed?

Statistical significance does not measure effect magnitude or practical importance. The American Statistical Association’s 2016 statement also emphasizes transparent reporting and interpretation beyond threshold decisions; see the source below.

Appendix F: Reproducibility record

Keep the following with a completed analysis:

  • Original inputs, data dictionary, provenance, and authorized access information.
  • Cleaning rules, exclusions, missingness decisions, and input checks.
  • The question, target estimate, analysis plan, and later changes.
  • Scripts, software versions, package environment, and random seeds where relevant.
  • Exported numeric results and figures used in the report.
  • AI prompts or suggestions that materially affected decisions, plus human checks.

In R, inspect documentation and record the environment locally:

help("t.test", package = "stats")
help("wilcox.test", package = "stats")
sessionInfo()
citation()

These are displayed commands for you to run, not executable book chunks. Prefer your installed-version help when checking actual behavior; online documentation may describe a different R release.

Statistical interpretation

American Statistical Association (2016). Statement announcement and six principles on statistical significance and p-values. This official document links to the published statement by Wasserstein and Lazar, The ASA Statement on p-Values: Context, Process, and Purpose, DOI: 10.1080/00031305.2016.1154108.

Use the statement for interpretation principles and the method-specific documentation for computational details. Verify any additional article, quotation, or AI-suggested citation before including it in a report.

Appendix H: Guide validation status

The developed chapters provide synthetic examples, scripts, and interpretation checks. Numerical examples were checked with independent Python calculations where reported during development. Such checks do not constitute execution of the R scripts.

R execution, generated R figures, and complete Quarto rendering still require validation in the target environment. Some chapters intentionally omit particular effect intervals or advanced procedures and state those limits explicitly. Simulation outputs in Chapter 20 must be obtained from the supplied R script before being reported as R results.

Before publishing the guide, run the scripts, inspect output tables and figures, render the assembled book, and resolve any broken links or discrepancies. Ensure the title page, Preface status, and method chapters describe the same release state.
